📖 John 1:1 (요한복음 1:1)
[1:1a] Ἐν ἀρχῇ ἦν ὁ λόγος (In the beginning was the Word)
[1:1b] καὶ ὁ λόγος ἦν πρὸς τὸν θεόν (and the Word was with God)
[1:1c] καὶ θεὸς ἦν ὁ λόγος (and the Word was God)
📌 [1:1a] Ἐν ἀρχῇ ἦν ὁ λόγος
  • Ἐν (In): Preposition taking the dative case, indicating time or state.
  • ἀρχῇ (beginning): Dative singular of ἀρχή. Anarthous (no article) after a preposition, denoting absolute, uncreated beginning.
  • ἦν (was): Imperfect active indicative 3rd person singular of εἰμί. Expresses continuous, ongoing existence in past time.
  • ὁ λόγος (the Word): Nominative singular with the definite article ὁ, acting as the subject.
Exegetical Insight: The imperfect tense ἦν emphasizes that when "the beginning" occurred, the Word was already in existence. It conveys the eternal, uncreated nature of Christ.
📌 [1:1b] καὶ ὁ λόγος ἦν πρὸς τὸν θεόν
  • καὶ (and): Coordinating conjunction.
  • ὁ λόγος (the Word): Subject with article.
  • ἦν (was): Imperfect active indicative.
  • πρὸς (with / toward): Preposition governing the accusative case (τὸν θεόν). Literally means "face-to-face with" or "directed toward."
  • τὸν θεόν (God): Accusative singular with the definite article τὸν, referring specifically to God the Father.
Exegetical Insight: Instead of μετά or σύν (simple accompaniment), John uses πρὸς, which denotes an active, intimate, face-to-face personal communion within the Godhead.
📌 [1:1c] καὶ θεὸς ἦν ὁ λόγος
  • θεὸς (God): Predicate nominative without article (anarthous), placed first in the clause for emphasis.
  • ἦν (was): Imperfect active indicative.
  • ὁ λόγος (the Word): Subject with the definite article ὁ.
Exegetical Insight: According to Colwell's Rule, an anarthous predicate nominative preceding the verb stresses qualitative nature/essence. Having ὁ before λόγος establishes it as the subject, while the lack of article before θεὸς distinguishes the Person of the Son from the Father while fully affirming His essential Deity.
📖 John 1:2 (요한복음 1:2)
[1:2a] οὗτος ἦν ἐν ἀρχῇ (This One was in the beginning)
[1:2b] πρὸς τὸν θεόν (with God)
📌 [1:2a-b] οὗτος ἦν ἐν ἀρχῇ πρὸς τὸν θεόν
  • οὗτος (This One): Demonstrative pronoun, nominative masculine singular, referring back to ὁ λόγος.
  • ἦν (was): Imperfect active indicative 3rd person singular.
  • ἐν ἀρχῇ (in the beginning): Prepositional phrase specifying time/state.
  • πρὸς τὸν θεόν (with God): Prepositional phrase denoting personal relation with the Father.
Exegetical Insight: Reaffirms that the Word's personal relationship with the Father was established prior to all created history, joining His eternal existence with His eternal fellowship.
📖 John 1:3 (요한복음 1:3)
[1:3a] πάντα δι’ αὐτοῦ ἐγένετο (All things came into being through Him)
[1:3b] καὶ χωρὶς αὐτοῦ ἐγένετο οὐδὲ ἕν (and apart from Him came into being not even one thing)
[1:3c] ὃ γέγονεν (that has come into being)
📌 [1:3a] πάντα δι’ αὐτοῦ ἐγένετο
  • πάντα (all things): Nominative neuter plural of πᾶς. (Neuter plural subjects in Greek regularly take a singular verb).
  • δι’ [διά] (through): Preposition with genitive (αὐτοῦ), denoting agency or instrumentality.
  • ἐγένετο (came into being): Aorist middle indicative 3rd person singular of γίνομαι. Denotes a historical point-in-time origination (creation).
Exegetical Insight: Contrasts ἐγένετο (came into being / created) with ἦν (was / eternally existing) in verses 1–2. Christ Himself is the uncreated Creator through whom all created things originated.
📌 [1:3b-c] καὶ χωρὶς αὐτοῦ ἐγένετο οὐδὲ ἕν ὃ γέγονεν
  • χωρὶς (apart from / without): Preposition taking the genitive (αὐτοῦ).
  • οὐδὲ ἕν (not even one thing): Emphatic negation (οὐδέ + ἕν).
  • ὃ (that / which): Relative pronoun nominative neuter singular.
  • γέγονεν (has come into being): Perfect active indicative 3rd person singular of γίνομαι. Denotes past completion with ongoing present results.
Exegetical Insight: The emphatic double statement (positive then negative) absolutely excludes any possibility of self-existent matter or independent creation apart from Christ.
